## The challenge

Riverside Literacy Project sent one monthly newsletter to its entire list of roughly 4,000 contacts: donors, volunteers, parents and lapsed supporters all got the same email. Open rates had slid to 24%, and nobody could say which emails led to donations.

## Approach

**1. Clean and segment.** I audited the list, suppressed 600+ contacts who hadn't opened anything in 18 months, and built four segments in HubSpot: active donors, lapsed donors, volunteers and families.

**2. Build the lifecycle.** I created three automated journeys:

- A **welcome series** (3 emails) for new subscribers, telling the story of one student reader.
- A **thank-you and impact flow** triggered by a donation.
- A **win-back sequence** for donors who hadn't given in 12 months.

**3. Measure what matters.** I tagged every link with UTMs and built a small Looker Studio dashboard so the team could see email-to-donation conversion for the first time.

## Results

After one quarter:

- Average open rate **rose from 24% to 46%**, and CTR from 2.1% to 6.8%.
- **Online donations up 27%** quarter over quarter, with email now the second-largest traffic source to the donate page.
- The win-back sequence reactivated **38 lapsed donors** in its first two months.

## What I learned

The list cleanup felt like it would hurt our numbers. Instead it was the single biggest lever on deliverability. Sending fewer, better-targeted emails beat sending more.
