## The challenge

Every April the Office of Sustainability ran an Earth Week campaign from its own Instagram account, and every year it reached roughly the same few hundred people who already followed it. The Student Marketing Association was asked to help it reach everyone else.

## Insight

Students don't follow university departments, but they do follow each other. If the message came from friends and teammates rather than an office, it would travel further and feel less like a lecture.

## Strategy

- **Recruit messengers, not followers.** We recruited 40 ambassadors from clubs, Greek life and athletics, prioritizing reach outside the "already green" crowd.
- **One simple ask.** Instead of ten tips, one pledge: bring a reusable cup for the rest of the semester. Signing up took about 15 seconds.
- **Make participation visible.** Pledgers got a sticker and a story template, so the campaign spread through the same feeds it was trying to reach.

## Execution

I managed the ambassador program end to end: the recruiting form, a one-page brief with talking points and do's and don'ts, a shared asset folder, and a tracking sheet with UTM links for every ambassador so we could see who was driving sign-ups. I also edited the launch video in CapCut.

## Results

- **1,150 pledges** in one week, against a goal of 500.
- Campaign content reached **3.1× more accounts** than the previous year's Earth Week, for a 210% increase.
- The top 5 ambassadors drove 44% of sign-ups. We now recruit ambassadors by network reach, not enthusiasm alone.
- Dining services reported a measurable rise in reusable-cup discounts the following month.

## What I learned

UTM links per ambassador were the best decision we made. They turned "it felt like it went well" into a ranked list we could act on, and they gave the ambassadors friendly competition.
