## The question

Streaming services spend heavily to acquire young subscribers, and then lose them fast. Our team set out to understand *why* students cancel, and what would keep them.

## Method

- **Qualitative first:** 10 in-depth interviews to build a vocabulary for how students actually talk about subscriptions.
- **Then quantitative:** a 24-question Qualtrics survey (n = 412), distributed through class group chats and student org newsletters, with quota checks by year and major.
- **Analysis:** cross-tabs and a k-means cluster analysis in SPSS on usage frequency, number of services and stated reasons for cancelling.

## Key findings

1. **Price wasn't the main driver.** Only 22% named price as their main reason for cancelling. The most common pattern was *subscription rotation*: subscribe for one show, binge it, cancel, then move on to the next service.
2. **Four segments emerged:** Loyalists (19%), Rotators (37%), Freeloaders on shared accounts (28%) and Minimalists (16%).
3. **Rotators planned ahead.** 61% said they'd stay subscribed if they knew what was coming next month.

## Recommendation

Rather than discounting, we proposed a **"coming next" retention program**: personalized, 30-day-ahead release calendars sent in the last week of a billing cycle, targeted at the Rotator segment.
