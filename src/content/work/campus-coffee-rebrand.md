## The challenge

The Grind is a café run by business students in the library basement. Mornings were packed; afternoons were empty. The managers assumed the fix was a discount. Our brief from the Brand Management course was to find out whether that was actually true.

## Research

We surveyed **214 students** and ran **12 short intercept interviews** outside the library between 1pm and 5pm. Price came up, but it wasn't the headline:

- **58%** of afternoon library users didn't know The Grind was open after noon.
- The people who did know described it as "the place you grab coffee on the way to class," not somewhere to stay.
- The real competitor wasn't another café. It was the vending machine on the second floor.

So this was an awareness and positioning problem, not a pricing problem.

## Strategy

We repositioned the afternoon as **"The Second Shift"**: a study-break ritual rather than a caffeine pit stop. Three moves:

1. **Say it where the decision happens.** Floor decals and table tents on the library's study floors, with a QR code to the menu.
2. **Give people a reason to come back.** A punch-card loyalty program, digitized through the café's existing Square POS, with double stamps from 2 to 5pm.
3. **Make the brand feel like the students.** A looser, funnier Instagram voice built around exam-season moments, plus a weekly "Second Shift Special" drink.

## Execution

I wrote the creative brief and the messaging framework, and I built the six-week content calendar. Two teammates produced the in-library materials; the fourth ran the survey analysis. We pitched the plan to the café's student managers in week 7, and they ran it for the rest of the semester.

## Results

Compared with the same six weeks the previous semester:

- Weekday **2–5pm transactions up 38%**, with no change to pricing.
- **612 loyalty sign-ups**, 41% of them redeemed at least once.
- Instagram engagement rate rose from **3.1% to 7.4%**; "Second Shift" posts outperformed the account average by 2.4×.

## What I learned

The most valuable hour of the project was the first round of intercept interviews. Our team walked in with a solution (a discount) and walked out with a different problem (awareness). I now try to earn the right to an idea with research before I fall in love with it.
